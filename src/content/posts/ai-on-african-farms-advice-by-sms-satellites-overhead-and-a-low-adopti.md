## The landscape shift

A 2026 systematic review of AI in East African agriculture identifies five main application areas: advisory services, precision agriculture, crop and pest monitoring, financial and market intelligence, and climate-smart agriculture. [[c1]](#src-c1)

In parts of East Africa, the review reports, farmers using digital and AI-supported advisory services reported better crop management, increased yields and reduced input waste. [[c3]](#src-c3)

## What is being built

### Advice on the phone

- Kenyan smallholders use tools including PlantVillage+ and Virtual Agronomist to get real-time advice on pest control, fertiliser use and crop disease detection. [[c5]](#src-c5)
- In Ghana and Nigeria, SMS-based AI systems give localised advice on planting dates, fertiliser use and pest control, and the magazine says farmers reported yield increases of between 30% and 50% when advice was combined with quality inputs. [[c7]](#src-c7)
- Kenya's iShamba farmer helpline serves more than 1.2 million farmers through text and voice services. [[c9]](#src-c9)
- Opportunity International signed with Safaricom to bring an AI chatbot, FarmerAI, to Kenya, piloting with potato farmers through DigiFarm, according to AVODA Group. [[c12]](#src-c12)
- A data scientist quoted by iAfrica says mobile tools such as WhatsApp bots could give smallholders real-time agronomic advice and address a shortage of agricultural extension officers. [[c6]](#src-c6)

### Eyes from above and in the field

- Nigeria's National Space Research and Development Agency, working with the Ministry of Agriculture, analyses satellite imagery every five days to map farm boundaries, monitor crop health and forecast national harvests months ahead. [[c8]](#src-c8)
- Femi Adekoya of Integrated Aerial Precision in Nigeria uses drones and AI to promote precision agriculture, according to the FAO. [[c11]](#src-c11)

## Limits of the evidence

The review finds that adoption of AI among smallholder farmers in East Africa remains relatively low and uneven. [[c2]](#src-c2) It links Kenya's lead and Ethiopia's low adoption to access to smartphones and digital agricultural services. [[c4]](#src-c4) An FAO event highlighted that digital inclusion is essential for rural communities. [[c10]](#src-c10)
