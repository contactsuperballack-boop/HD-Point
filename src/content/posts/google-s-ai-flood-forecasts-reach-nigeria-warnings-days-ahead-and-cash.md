## Why it matters

Urban flash flooding was the hard problem because sensors almost never monitor it, according to Streamline Feed. [[c8]](#src-c8) For these floods Google built Groundsource, which uses its Gemini AI to analyse historical news reports. [[c4]](#src-c4)

## What it does

### Two models for river floods

Flood Hub uses two integrated AI models: a hydrologic model that forecasts the amount of water flowing in a river, and an inundation model that predicts which areas will be affected and to what depth. [[c9]](#src-c9)

### Flash floods from news archives

Google says Groundsource reviewed more than 5 million reports spanning roughly 20 years and helped create a dataset of about 2.6 million flood events. [[c5]](#src-c5) It says the method helps forecast floods in areas that lack local sensors. [[c10]](#src-c10)

## In Nigeria

In Kogi, Nigeria, one of West Africa's most flood-affected regions, GiveDirectly used Google's Flood Forecasting API to deliver cash to people before the water rose. [[c6]](#src-c6) Google says GiveDirectly found that the early cash helped families evacuate, protect assets and rebuild. [[c7]](#src-c7)

## Availability

The forecasts are available through Flood Hub and also appear in Google Search when people look for information on a flood in their area. [[c3]](#src-c3) Researchers can access the predictions through Flood Hub, and can use open-source data and an API for their own disaster resilience research. [[c11]](#src-c11)
