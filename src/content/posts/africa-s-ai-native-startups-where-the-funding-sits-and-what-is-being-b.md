## The landscape shift

Funding is concentrated. Four markets took 85% of country-specific startup funding, up from 83% in 2025. [[c5]](#src-c5)

The money is also spreading. Tanzania, Côte d’Ivoire and Morocco each attracted more than $25 million in the first half of 2026. [[c7]](#src-c7)

## What is being built

### Voice and language

UNESCO estimates that Africa is home to between 1,500 and 3,000 languages, according to a profile of Botlhale AI. [[c8]](#src-c8)

- Botlhale AI, a South African startup, builds speech recognition and language tools for African languages, aimed at call centres. [[c9]](#src-c9)
- Botlhale says up to 70% of calls in South Africa are in languages other than English. [[c10]](#src-c10)
- The company says its models support 11 South African languages and it is expanding to Ghana, Kenya and Nigeria. [[c11]](#src-c11)

### Agriculture and climate

- Amini, a Kenyan startup, uses satellite imagery and AI to map environmental and agricultural data, including soil, vegetation and carbon levels. [[c12]](#src-c12)
- Aerobotics, a South African company, combines AI, drones and data analytics to help farmers monitor crop health. [[c13]](#src-c13)

## Limits of the data

The funding figures cover startups across all sectors, not AI startups alone. In the first half of 2026, fintech led at 41% ($556 million), followed by logistics and transport at 35% ($472 million) and agriculture and food at 7% ($93 million). [[c6]](#src-c6)

## What comes next

Africa: The Big Deal is expected to publish a consolidated 2026 funding review before year-end, which will show whether Egypt's 27% share of first-half funding holds. [[c14]](#src-c14)
