Talent Nation, a startup founded by Nigerian serial entrepreneur Iyinoluwa Aboyeji and South African-born entrepreneur Kesiah Valkin, has raised an undisclosed pre-seed round. [[c1]](#src-c1) It aims to train and deploy one million AI engineers from Africa into businesses worldwide over the next decade. [[c2]](#src-c2)

> **Key facts**
>
> - The round was co-led by Enza Capital and London-based LocalGlobe, with participation from Future Africa, Kaleo Ventures, Ajim Capital, Solving.org and Accelerate Africa. [[c3]](#src-c3)
> - The company has not disclosed the size of the round or a detailed timetable for reaching its target. [[c6]](#src-c6)
> - Its Learn2Earn academy trains, certifies and deploys engineers into organisations that want to build and manage their own AI capabilities. [[c4]](#src-c4)
> - Aboyeji told Semafor that Learn2Earn received 63,000 applications, and fewer than 3% were selected. [[c8]](#src-c8)

## The details

Aboyeji, the executive chairman, co-founded Andela and Flutterwave, according to Semafor. [[c5]](#src-c5) He says Talent Nation wants to make certified AI engineering talent accessible to organisations at a fraction of the cost of hiring from frontier AI labs or traditional consulting firms. [[c7]](#src-c7)

He told Semafor that most developers from the training programmes will be African, though he does not want to constrain the company to that. [[c9]](#src-c9)

## Market context

Companies are adopting AI quickly, but many struggle to find engineers who can build and maintain reliable systems around AI models, according to TechAwk NG. [[c11]](#src-c11) The venture mirrors Andela, which Aboyeji launched in 2014 and which trained a generation of software engineers in six African countries, according to Semafor. [[c10]](#src-c10)
