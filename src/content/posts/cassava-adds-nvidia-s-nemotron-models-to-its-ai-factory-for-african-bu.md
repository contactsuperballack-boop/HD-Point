> - The AI Factory was first announced in March 2025, with a stated aim of keeping data within Africa's borders. [[c5]](#src-c5)

## Why it matters

Cassava's chief technology and AI officer says expanding the AI Factory is about creating the capacity and economics businesses need to build and deploy AI at scale. [[c4]](#src-c4) The company says it targets researchers, startups and developers in sectors including health and fintech, and governments. [[c11]](#src-c11)

## What it does

### Models and computing

Cassava will offer Nvidia's Nemotron open models as a managed service on its AI Factory. [[c1]](#src-c1) The service gives African businesses API access to AI tools and managed computing. [[c2]](#src-c2)

### Agent security

Cassava is also using Nvidia OpenShell to combine computing, controlled agent activity and independent security enforcement, as AI agents gain access to business systems. [[c3]](#src-c3)

## How it got here

- March 2025: Cassava first announced the AI Factory, with data centres in South Africa, Egypt, Kenya, Morocco and Nigeria in line for upgrades with Nvidia hardware. [[c5]](#src-c5)
- Bloomberg reported that Cassava could invest up to $720 million in the project. [[c6]](#src-c6)
- South Africa was to receive the first 3,000 Nvidia GPUs by June 2025. [[c7]](#src-c7)
- November 2025: Cassava launched what it called Africa's first GPU-as-a-service. [[c8]](#src-c8)
- Founder Strive Masiyiwa said the company had acquired 12,000 Nvidia GPUs. [[c9]](#src-c9)
- Cassava agreed to provide computing capacity to Rockefeller Foundation grantees across eight countries, including Kenya. [[c10]](#src-c10)
