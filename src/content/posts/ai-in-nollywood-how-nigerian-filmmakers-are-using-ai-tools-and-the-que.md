## The landscape shift

Nigerian filmmakers and digital creators are using AI to cut costs, expand storytelling possibilities and compete globally, industry insiders told Forbes Africa. [[c1]](#src-c1)

In March 2026, a YouTube-backed workshop in Lagos introduced filmmakers including Biodun Stephen and Omoni Oboli to Flow, an AI filmmaking tool developed by Google. [[c2]](#src-c2) Filmmakers there said AI could help them visualise scenes before filming, speed up conceptualisation and simulate scenes that would be expensive or difficult to shoot. [[c3]](#src-c3)

## How filmmakers are using it

### On set and in post

- Actor and producer Omoni Oboli says she has used AI tools to generate story ideas, animate videos and create promotional materials. [[c4]](#src-c4)
- For the film Eshugbayi Eleko, AI was used to create establishing shots and briefly reconstruct Lagos as it looked in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, with some AI-generated sequences lasting only a few seconds, a filmmaker told NewsCrackers NG. [[c6]](#src-c6)
- Travel and lifestyle creator Tayo Aina says he has used generative AI to recreate historical moments in his documentary-style videos when archival footage was unavailable. [[c5]](#src-c5)

### On screen and at festivals

- Makemation, produced by Nigerian AI developer turned filmmaker Toyosi Akerele-Ogunsiji, was released in Nigerian cinemas in 2025 and has toured international festivals and universities. [[c9]](#src-c9)
- Its producers describe AI as both a storytelling subject and part of the production workflow, combining generative AI systems with live-action filmmaking. [[c10]](#src-c10)
- The Naija Artificial Intelligence Film Festival, founded by Obinna Okerekeocha, received 490 submissions for its debut, according to TechCabal. [[c7]](#src-c7)

## Limits

NewsCrackers NG says transparency about AI use could become increasingly important in Nollywood as audiences encounter more AI-generated images, voices and scenes. [[c11]](#src-c11)

## What comes next

Okerekeocha says he wants to train thousands of African AI filmmakers over the next five years and support tools trained on local languages, voices and cultures. [[c8]](#src-c8)
