## The landscape shift

Nigeria drafted the strategy with its AI community. A four-day workshop in Abuja brought together over 120 AI researchers and practitioners, according to the ministry. [[c3]](#src-c3) A draft was published in August 2024, [[c4]](#src-c4) and the strategy was launched in Lagos in April 2025. [[c1]](#src-c1)

## What the strategy sets out

### The five pillars

The strategy is built on five pillars: building AI infrastructure, fostering an innovation ecosystem, driving AI adoption across sectors, ensuring responsible AI development, and establishing robust governance. [[c2]](#src-c2)

### Early steps

- Short-term items include setting up governance bodies, seeding demonstration projects, a national AI skills drive and pilot high-performance computing infrastructure. [[c6]](#src-c6)
- It cites a target to equip 70% of youth with AI-related skills. [[c5]](#src-c5)
- In 2024 the ministry said Galaxy Backbone would partner with 21st Century Technologies on a national compute project open to local researchers, startups and government entities working on AI projects of national interest. [[c13]](#src-c13)

## Limits of the evidence

The Tunani policy brief finds the strategy has few binding commitments, measurable targets or dedicated budget lines. [[c7]](#src-c7) It says Nigeria has advanced on talent, through the 3MTT programme and the N-ATLAS language model, but lags peers on infrastructure, governance and R&D spending. [[c10]](#src-c10)

## What comes next

- Minister Bosun Tijani has presented a vision for a National AI Trust at a convening hosted by Warwick Business School in London, describing it as the first institution of its kind in the world. [[c11]](#src-c11)
- He is also raising $2 billion for Project BRIDGE, an initiative to lay 90,000 kilometres of fibre-optic cable across Nigeria. [[c12]](#src-c12)
