> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Nigeria unveiled N-ATLAS, an open-source language model, on the sidelines of the 80th UN General Assembly in September 2025. [[c1]](#src-c1)
> - It supports Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Nigerian-accented English. [[c2]](#src-c2)
> - The National Centre for Artificial Intelligence and Robotics built it with Awarri Technologies. [[c3]](#src-c3)
> - The language model is fine-tuned from Llama-3 8B and reportedly trained on more than 400 million tokens. [[c5]](#src-c5)[[c6]](#src-c6)

## Why it matters

The government first announced the project in April 2024. [[c4]](#src-c4) Awarri says developers, researchers and innovators can use N-ATLAS to build tools for education, governance and health. [[c10]](#src-c10)

## What it does

### Speech and captions

The release includes language-specific speech recognition models that can transcribe radio, television and online video into captions and subtitles. [[c7]](#src-c7)

### The language model

N-ATLaS-LLM is fine-tuned from Llama-3 8B and designed to support African languages. [[c5]](#src-c5) It is reported to have been trained on more than 400 million tokens of multilingual instruction data. [[c6]](#src-c6)

## Availability

Documentation for the model is published on Hugging Face. [[c8]](#src-c8)

## Limits

TechCabal reports that the launch's significance depends on whether researchers, developers and startups can turn it into practical tools for classrooms, clinics and farms, despite funding and infrastructure gaps. [[c9]](#src-c9)

## What comes next

Awarri says it is launching a locally trained, voice-first AI assistant that converses in Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Pidgin. [[c11]](#src-c11)
